Two users, one flow

Document capture, biometric checks, and manual review: a calm user flow and a fast analyst dashboard on one design system.

Calm users: one job per screen

I learned Anxiety dominated early research; users lost confidence on uncertain screens.
So I changed Single-purpose screens: document capture apart from biometrics, with progress, a clear success state, and a confirmation before leaving.
Evaluated via Self-review against the wireframes: each screen asks for one thing and shows what's next.

Analysts: sortable queues

I learned Analysts triage by risk and age; a flat list made that harder.
So I changed Filterable, sortable queues with status and priority at a glance, plus a separate settings area.
Evaluated via Self-review: priority is readable without opening a case.

Analysts: three actions

I learned My first review screen showed every action at once, slowing analysts.
So I changed Three primary actions (Reject, Escalate, Approve); reasons, confirmations and notes move to an overlay.
Evaluated via Early low-fi vs. revised dashboard: fewer decisions per case, no action lost.

Fewer visible choices per case trades screen detail for analyst speed; the overlay keeps every action an analyst needs.

Four personas, two audiences

Two end users and two analysts. They kept both interfaces grounded.

R

Riley

The Anxious Verifier · User, age 25

Cautious with technology and second-guesses every step, especially when personal information is involved. Mobile-first, moderate tech comfort, often multitasking in imperfect conditions (poor lighting, noise).

Needs

  • Clear, simple instructions
  • Reassurance their information is safe
  • Predictable steps, no surprises
  • Helpful guidance during document capture

Pain points

  • Confusing instructions
  • Fear of submitting the wrong photo
  • Unclear progress indicators
  • Stress around sharing personal information

Goal Complete verification quickly and confidently without feeling confused or worried.

J

Jordan

The Busy Multitasker · User, age 25

Expects modern apps to be fast, intuitive, and polished. Anything slow or unclear feels like wasted time. Tech-savvy, uses multiple devices, often on the go.

Needs

  • Fast, frictionless flow
  • Clear progress indicators
  • Responsive design across mobile and desktop
  • Minimal steps, no repeated actions

Pain points

  • Slow loading screens
  • Repeating steps due to unclear instructions
  • Poor mobile responsiveness
  • Lack of visual polish

Goal Finish verification quickly with minimal friction and move on with their day.

C

Casey

The Speed-Focused Reviewer · Analyst, age 38

Reviews a high volume of verification cases daily and prefers clean layouts that support quick decisions. Works in operations or compliance, high computer literacy, values speed and accuracy.

Needs

  • Clear hierarchy of information
  • Quick access to key details
  • Consistent patterns across cases
  • Minimal visual noise

Pain points

  • Cluttered dashboards
  • Inconsistent layouts
  • Too many clicks to find essential info
  • Cognitive overload from dense screens

Goal Review cases quickly and confidently, without unnecessary steps or clutter.

M

Morgan

The Detail-Oriented Verifier · Analyst, age 45

Handles complex or flagged cases and relies on clear, organized information to spot discrepancies. Works in risk, fraud, or compliance. Highly detail-oriented, prefers clarity over speed.

Needs

  • Organized, structured case information
  • Strong visual hierarchy
  • Clear separation of sections
  • Easy access to supporting documents

Pain points

  • Poorly organized case data
  • Hard-to-read document previews
  • Inconsistent formatting
  • Missing or unclear labels

Goal Use a clear, structured dashboard that surfaces issues fast and supports accurate decisions.

Paper to pixels

Structure settled before style.

One token system

A neutral base plus accent, success, and error colors in five tints each.

  • Five-stop color ramps (100 → 900) for accent, positive, and negative feedback
  • Status color mapped consistently to meaning across every button, badge, and banner
  • One shared type and spacing scale across both interfaces

Outcome

Self-initiated: the goal was a defensible process, not a shipped metric.

Lesson: designing for two audiences on one flow means deciding which parts of the system flex per audience and which never do.
