WHY THIS CHANGE (background for Eric)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each case-study page (SecureID, Honda, DevTective, Vextaro) renders as a
slide deck too, exactly like the Hero section — you click or arrow
through numbered slides. Two things are changing, matching what we just
did to Hero:

1) Every slide in these decks currently starts with a small gold caption
   like "01 — The problem" above its heading. That's being removed, the
   same way Hero's meta label was removed — the heading itself should
   carry the idea on its own, and the slide-rail thumbnails (which
   already say things like "Empathizing: what we learned," "Personas,"
   "Impact & Reflection") are staying exactly as they are — those are
   good and don't need to change.

2) The body paragraph text in every section (15px right now) is growing
   to match Hero's new body-text size and font. This is a ONE-TIME shared
   CSS change — one CSS rule in assets/css/style.css controls this for
   all 4 case-study pages at once, so it only needs to be made here, in
   this first case-study file. Note it does NOT touch the opening/cover
   slide of each case study (the one with the project title, tagline, and
   Role/Team/Scope/Tools grid) — that slide uses different styling
   entirely and is intentionally left untouched.

On top of the structural change, SecureID's text is being fully
rewritten: shorter headings a recruiter can skim in a glance, tighter
paragraphs, and one content fix — the "Personas" section currently
displays 3 of your 4 personas crammed into number-styled stat boxes
(visually implying they're statistics, which they aren't). That's being
replaced with a plain, readable list of all 4 personas. Every fact,
number, and quote below is already on the live page today — nothing new
is being invented, only reorganized and tightened. The reasoning-chain
blocks ("We learned / So I changed / Evaluated via") and the outcome
stats at the bottom are strong as written and are being kept, just with
shorter section headings around them.

        checks can't resolve every case, so a human analyst has to step in — and the interface
        that got the <em>user</em> through verification smoothly wasn't built to help an analyst
        triage a queue of flagged cases quickly.

      <p>

        that needed to feel trustworthy and low-friction for the end user, and a case-review
